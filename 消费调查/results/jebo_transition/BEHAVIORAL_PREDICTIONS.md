# Behavioral predictions and identification limits

Conceptual synthesis only. No structural estimation or new tests. A finite windfall spending share is not automatically a local derivative of a consumption function. Predictions below require the stated auxiliary conditions.

| Account | Cash level / size gradient | Restricted level / gap as size rises | Extensive versus conditional/high response | Existing diagnostic | Missing measurement / conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Concave consumption / permanent-income benchmark | Level depends on horizon and resources. Concavity of the relevant finite-horizon consumption function can imply a falling finite-gain share; permanent-income reasoning alone does not fix it. | Equal under full fungibility and matched timing; otherwise gap ambiguous. | Both margins depend on heterogeneity and adjustment thresholds. | Declining Cash midpoint and ordinal scores are compatible. | No common horizon, wealth or structural preferences; not a test of all standard theory. |
| Liquidity / hand-to-mouth | High local response near a constraint; a larger gain can leave that region. | Lower spendability can reduce response; category substitution can undo restriction. Gap ambiguous. | Participation and conditional amount can both change. | Existing income and emergency-funding associations only. | Income is not liquid wealth; no exogenous liquidity manipulation. |
| Small-windfall mental accounting | Small cash may enter a current-spending account; larger cash may be allocated to assets. A decline requires this categorization assumption. | Earmarked labels may reduce small-amount response; gap may shrink, but no universal sign. | Lower conditional shares/high responses possible without more zero reports. | Decomposition consistent with that pattern. | No intended-use, spendability or account-categorization mediator. |
| Attention / finite planning horizon | Size can trigger adjustment and lengthen planning. | Expiry or salient uses can alter attention/horizon; gap ambiguous. | Larger shocks can increase positive-response frequency while reducing conditional shares. | Cash participation is descriptively stable; no decisive rejection. | Planning, attention and common time horizon absent. |
| Mechanical category restriction | Cash is a flexible benchmark. | Inframarginal eligible spending can make restrictions irrelevant; bindingness can increase with size. Total-spending gap not universally monotone with heterogeneous substitution or timing. | Both entry and share can change; floor arithmetic is insufficient to isolate cause. | Food triple contrast Holm7=.0504; low-expenditure group shows larger differential slope. | Coarse baseline food bands, six-month extrapolation and unmeasured counterfactual eligibility. Restrictions not excluded. |
| Earmarking / commitment / category budgets | Baseline cash depends on self-control and intended purchases. | Restricted balances may substitute for budgets or induce additional purchases; gap may shrink, widen or reverse. | Entry and conditional shares both possible; aggregate near-zero change can hide offsets. | Medical components offset descriptively; Food crossover uncertain. | Attributes bundled; no direct allocation or commitment measure. |

The prediction exercise reduces overstatement, not uncertainty by assertion. None of the six accounts is uniquely identified. Relative-scale fit comparisons and observational moderation are diagnostics, not a causal mediation chain. An account is not refuted simply because one coefficient is nonsignificant, and agreement with a flexible prediction is not confirmation.
