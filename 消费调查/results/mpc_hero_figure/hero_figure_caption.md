# Hero figure | Transfer size reshapes stated spending responses differently across transfer forms

**A,** Full six-category stated-MPC distributions in the nine randomized form-by-amount cells. Each equally long strip represents 100% of that cell; segment widths are the approved unconditional category shares, ordered from “basically no additional spending” to spending more than 75% of the transfer. Six discrete shades encode response intensity, not transfer form. Cash's >75% share falls from 13.4% at ¥200 to 5.3% at ¥5,000; the corresponding endpoint declines are smaller for Food and Medical. Cash's no-additional-spending share is almost unchanged (27.3% and 27.2%). **B,** The same cells summarized using midpoint-coded stated MPC (category scores 0, .05, .175, .375, .625 and .875). Points and thin error bars reproduce the approved cell means and pointwise 95% bootstrap percentile confidence intervals from the frozen results (5,000 cell-stratified resamples); none are re-estimated for this figure. Lines connect only the three discrete randomized amounts; they are not a fitted continuous dose–response curve.

Full approved sample: N=5,497; cell sizes 584–636. Respondents were randomized to Cash, Food/daily-necessities vouchers or Medical-account transfers at ¥200, ¥1,000 or ¥5,000 and each answered once. Cells therefore contain different people: the atlas does not depict within-person transitions. These are hypothetical stated responses, not realized MPC. Midpoint coding is a descriptive cardinal approximation; the original ordinal analysis remains a required companion reference. The visibly large Cash high-tail change does not establish exclusive tail-specificity or a general scaling law; frozen cross-threshold multiplicity-adjusted tests do not establish tail-specificity, and the mean/ordinal form-by-size interpretation remains suggestive. No new inference is attached to the composition strips.
