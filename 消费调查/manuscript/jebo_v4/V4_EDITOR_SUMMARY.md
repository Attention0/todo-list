# Editor-facing summary

The manuscript asks whether the form of a windfall matters equally at small and large amounts. A randomized survey experiment with 5,480 adults crosses Cash, Food-voucher and Medical-account scenarios with RMB 200, 1,000 and 5,000. Three observed puzzles organize the paper: a falling Cash mean, a smaller Food decline, and an almost flat Medical mean bring average shares closer at the large amount.

The substantive addition is distributional. Cash's highest-response category contracts while its bottom frequency barely changes; Food redistributes more diffusely; Medical combines offsetting category changes. Similar large-amount means therefore conceal different response profiles. Putting implied yuan beside shares clarifies that this is an allocation-share pattern, not a collapse in additional spending.

The explanation proceeds from consumption smoothing and scale to bottom-category arithmetic, a sharp Food bindingness prediction, category needs, relative resources and observable types. The Food sharp prediction has the opposite observed sign. Other diagnostics are precision-limited. Resource categorization and perceived spendability connect the puzzles as a candidate interpretation for a future experiment, not as an identified mediator.

The statistical boundary remains explicit: Cash midpoint and highest-category trends survive the 42-test adjustment, while cross-form interactions remain uncertain. The design uses hypothetical responses and bundles eligibility, expiry, liquidity and account structure. It cannot establish realized spending effects, a population multiplier or a pure label effect.

The paper's JEBO position is a behavioral question about how differently described resources enter allocation decisions, connected directly to Lee et al. (2024) and Pauls and Laudi (2025), and differentiated from Bernard's existing form-by-size design. Recruitment, fieldwork, allocation implementation, ethics/consent and author declarations still require verified author records before submission.

## Title selection after completion of the full draft

1. **Transfer form and the allocation of small and large windfalls** — selected: substantive question and bounded scope; does not claim a proven interaction or mediator.
2. Small and large windfalls across cash and earmarked resources — clear scope, but less focused on the allocation question.
3. Three paths toward similar spending shares — captures the anatomy, but could imply population convergence without qualification.
4. Transfer size form and the distribution of stated spending — precise, but puts method before the economic question.
5. When resource form meets transfer size — readable, but less explicit about the economic outcome.

The provisional working title was retained only after comparing these five alternatives against the completed draft. Avoid “Evidence for spendability,” “identified categorization,” or a universal cash-versus-in-kind ranking.
